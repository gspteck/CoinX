Crypto market basics: how prices, order books and exchanges work

New to reading a crypto market? This hub collects CoinX’s 20 educational guides on crypto market basics, in reading order: how an order book, spread and slippage work, how Bitcoin and Ethereum trade on exchanges, how altcoin liquidity differs, which market analysis techniques beginners use, and how to choose a trading platform. Everything here is educational, not financial advice, and CoinX is not an exchange.

Two terms come up in almost every guide. A market order fills straight away at the best price available, which may differ from the last price you saw. A limit order fills only at your chosen price or better, so it may fill only partly or not at all. Both definitions come from Investor.gov, the investor education site of the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, and they apply the same way on crypto exchanges.

Five terms to know first

Spread: the gap between the highest bid and the lowest ask. A wide spread is a cost you pay every time you cross it with a market order.

Depth: how much is offered or wanted at each price level near the top of the order book. Shallow depth means a larger order moves the price against you.

Slippage: the difference between the price you expected and the average price you actually got. It grows when books are thin or prices move fast.

Liquidity: how easily a coin can be bought or sold without moving its price. Bitcoin and Ether pairs are usually far more liquid than small altcoins on the same exchange.

Volume: how much traded over a period. Volume is useful context, but a single number on its own does not say whether a market is healthy or which way it will move next.
